# World inventory

docs/PLAN.md M4 #4, at upstream OpenSWOS `df643c2` (2026-11-21).

This document lists every place where upstream plays differently by world, and every fact the
original game hardcodes about its football world. Each is mapped to what our engine reads
instead: a field of the world package, or a behaviour component that the package names
(docs/PLAN.md §5.2, D6 and D7). It is the design the next steps build on:

- `world_rules` (M2 #8) reads the `rules/` files of §4;
- `world_extract` (M4 #5) writes them (§9);
- the competition and career ports (M5, M7) implement the components of §5.

## 1. How upstream picks a world

**Three worlds** (include/world.h `WorldId`):

- `WORLD_9697` (0): the original game.
- `WORLD_2627`: World 2026-27, the SWOS 96/97 Mod's.
- `WORLD_8485`: Europe 1984-85, the Mod's classic world, built on DaniSwos's database.

The plan knew two. The third arrived on 2026-11-13 (upstream docs/WORLD_2026_27.md §6g).
Like 2026-27, its data is never in upstream's git: it is a package hosted apart. Decision: §3.1.

**Two kinds of branch:**

- **Direct checks** of `World_Current()` against a world: 116 at this pin, in 31 files. The
  baseline `ec2ed2be` had 46, in 15 files. 46 of today's are in the generated tables, which
  check again what `world_baked.c` already dispatched.
- **Data gates**: about 120 accessors and hooks (`World_*`, `WorldPlayoff_*`, `WorldCups_*`…,
  in include/world*.h) whose data is absent in 96/97 (NULL, count 0), so the original code runs.
  `src/world_baked.c` (`WORLD_CALL`) answers each from the current world's generated table.
  Upstream's rule (docs/WORLD_2026_27.md §6b): new world code "gates on the world's data or
  `World_Current()`".

**Two layers of data**, in this order (frontend.c `FrontEndPreLoopSetup`):

1. The **baked tables**: `world_data.c` and `world_data_8485.c`, generated by
   `scripts/world_import.py`. They are installed over 96/97's compiled records.
2. On SDL, the **JSON overlay** from `data_json/worlds/<tag>/` (save_format_json.c). A team file
   is replaced whole, and a competition record is replaced by `id`. Some files are never
   overlaid: countries, country competitions, qualifying, league blocks, seed buckets and the
   career index. A world's new countries reach the game only through the baked tables.
   The merge rules are in world_data.md, "The JSON overlay".

**Upstream's 96/97** runs the original season end, with every world module's hook inert. It is
not quite the original game:

- it plays OpenSWOS's Copa Libertadores (§3.2);
- it is built with the worlds' capacities (2,560 global team numbers, 256 clubs a team file, a
  team cache of 160: `SWOS_WORLDS`, include/swos_types.h), while its saves keep the original's
  shape (save_format_json.c `emitted_len`).

  component, never to a world.

## 3. Decisions for the owner

All three taken as recommended on 2026-11-21: docs/PLAN.md D10, D11, D12.

### 3.1 Europe 1984-85 (decided: D10)

A third world, from the same Mod: 32 countries, 949 clubs, 32 national cups, the European cups
as knockouts (32 / 32 / 64), and a national cycle of the era. Like World 2026-27 its data is
not ours to redistribute, and upstream hosts its package apart (`PROJECT_WORLD*_URL`,
include/project_identity.h).

**Recommendation:** in scope, as a third downloaded package, once World 1996-97 and World
2026-27 play. This inventory already maps its branches. Most of what it needs, World 2026-27
needs too. Only four implementations are its own (§5): `no_same_country_early`,
`knockout_places`, `fixed_cycle_groups` and `cycle_places_then_direct`. Upstream has three
golden careers for it (ENG8485-LIVERPOOL, ITA8485-JUVENTUS, GDR8485-DRESDEN).

### 3.2 The Copa Libertadores in World 1996-97 (decided: D11)

Upstream's 96/97 plays OpenSWOS's Copa: the `_copaLibertadores` record is in its
`tournaments.json`, so it is already in our `data/worlds/1996-97/`. `career_copa.c` qualifies
the top two of 10 CONMEBOL leagues whenever the record is there and Brazil is a season country.
The Copa adds a competition to the season end, with its draws and simulations, and 20 clubs to
the season end's count checks.

**Recommendation:** keep it. World 1996-97 reproduces upstream's 96/97 path, which is what the
oracle and the golden careers test. Here the Copa is data: its record, and the `copa` block of
`rules/continental_cups.json`. A package without the two is the pure original.

### 3.3 Where `career_params.json` lives (decided: D12)

Upstream loads it for every world. No world changes it: the worlds change the economy through
their own modules (`rules/finance.json`, §4).

**Recommendation:** move it into each package, as `rules/career_params.json` with upstream's
schema. Packages are self-contained, and the plan counts the career economy as world data. For
World 1996-97 it is upstream's file as it is. The completion step copies the same file into
2026-27 and 1984-85, since upstream plays them with it.

## 7. The season end, step by step

This is `season.json` `end.steps`, from upstream's order (original_season.md has each step's
lines). The steps in brackets are components or modules that do nothing when the package has
none.

1. Load the league state into the scratch tables.
2. Draw next season's European eligibility (`EuropeanQualification`: the seeded draw for 16,
   32 and 32, in that order; an accepted job offer counts).
3. Prepare the Copa's list (`ContinentalClubCups`).
4. A national coach's competitions: European Championship, then World Cup
   (`NationalQualifyingDraw`, `NationalFinalsField`).
5. Finish the European cups: Champions' Cup, Cup Winners' Cup, UEFA Cup. Each is played if it
   is the career club's, else simulated; its winner is kept as holder. [the UEFA Super Cup takes
   the holders]
6. Finish the Copa.
7. Empty the team pool.
8. Each country's season, in `end.countries`' order, the career club's own country first
   (`end.own_country_first`):
   1. its league, division by division, played or simulated; division 0 caches the European
      candidates [play-off tables kept, early tables restored];
   2. the division play-offs (`LeaguePlayoffs`);
   3. [the league's Super Cup entrants];
   4. the national cup [the cup's Super Cup entrants];
   5. the league cup, and its European place (`national_cups.json`);
   6. the sum check of the moves;
   7. [the world's play-offs and play-outs, last season's titles, state championships];
   8. the country's European qualification (`EuropeanQualification`) [the other
      confederations see its table];
   9. its Copa qualification.
9. [The qualifying rounds fill the cups (`uefa_access_list`); the other confederations' next
   editions.]
10. The pool check: the clubs listed must be what qualification appends (80 in 96/97, plus the
    Copa's 20).
11. The holders join (`EuropeanHolderEntry`).
12. The count check: each cup holds its record's clubs (16 / 32 / 32 in 96/97). Upstream panics
    (0x8999, 0x8998); ours is an error.
13. Record the season's moves [cups' seasons counted; the career club's qualifying ties].
14. A new club or a new budget.
15. The new season: counters; [parallel groups' moves]; the copy-back of the league tables;
    competition slots from the country's table (league, national cup, league cup, continental
    in `start.continental_order`, play-off); [FFP, Serie D cup rounds, the Super Cup tie, the
    cup record].

The engine owns the screens around these steps: the retirement check, the chairman's verdict,
and the mods' hooks.

## 8. Limits

Upstream's capacities are fixed per build, not per world: the worlds' build has the larger
ones. 96/97 runs with them too (`SWOS_WORLDS`). In Rust, collections take their size from the
data, so a limit matters only where behaviour depends on it:

- **Saves:** the 2,000 league offsets a 96/97 save writes.
- **Load errors**, where upstream panics: a team number past the last (0x8F02), an odd league
  (0x1313), the season end's counts.
- **Quirks:** the original's 64,000-byte team-file buffer (93 clubs).

`limits.json` states each such capacity per package:

| limit | original (96/97 saves) | worlds' build |
|---|---|---|
| global team numbers | 2,000 | 2,560 |
| clubs a team file | 93 | 256 |
| team cache | 100 | 160 |
| divisions a league | 4 | 16 |
| countries a region list | 44 | 56 |
| European places a season end | 33 | 40 |
| TMD pool records | 128 | 160 |
| nationality codes | 153 | 223 |
| slots a country (two team files) | 256 | 512 |
| career contests | 48 (34 compiled) | 48 |

The world modules' own capacities are listed in each area file's Summary: levels, groups,
states, cup lists, Super Cups, conti tables, coefficient clubs, access list, cup record,
benches, B-team call-ups, extras, pools.

## 9. Completion (M4 #5)

`world_extract` builds each package whole. Upstream layers a world over 96/97 at start-up;
the completion step does that merge once, ahead of time.

**World 1996-97** (`data/worlds/1996-97/`, then assembled in `target/cache/worlds/1996-97/`):

- `competitions/`: upstream's files, as committed.
- `rules/`:
